How We Document Every Job

What Goes Into a Project Record

This page will fill up with completed jobs. Rather than put placeholders here, this is what each entry will contain — and what we document on your job whether or not it ends up on this page.

1

Where and what

The city, the type of property, and the laundry setup — a stacked closet unit in a Delray condo and a second-floor laundry in a Wellington house are different problems.

2

What the homeowner reported

In their words. Two cycles to dry, a hot laundry room, no air at the hood. The complaint is the starting point for the diagnosis.

3

What we found

The actual condition of the run: where the restriction was, what the duct was made of, whether anything was damaged or disconnected.

4

What we performed

The work itself, including anything we could not reach and why. Scope limits belong in the record as much as the work does.

5

What changed

The airflow reading before and after. Where we measured it, we publish it. Where we did not, we say so instead of implying a result.

6

Photographs

Before and after images of the duct, the termination and what came out of them, where conditions allowed us to take them.

In the meantime, the service pages describe the same work in detail: dryer vent cleaning, blockage removal, roof dryer vents and repairs.
