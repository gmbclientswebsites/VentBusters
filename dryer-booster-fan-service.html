Dryer Booster Fan Service

Long duct runs are sometimes built with an in-line booster fan to help the dryer push air the distance. Nobody thinks about them until drying times climb — and a fan sitting in the lint stream needs cleaning just as much as the duct around it.

What a Booster Fan Does

Extra Push for a Run That Is Too Long on Its Own

A dryer’s own blower is sized for a duct of modest length. Past a certain run — and the number of turns counts toward it — the machine cannot move air the whole distance effectively. A booster fan is an in-line fan installed partway along the duct to add that push.

Most of the ones we come across were installed with the house, usually on interior or second-floor laundry rooms with long attic runs out to a roof cap. They are typically wired to switch on when the dryer runs, either by a pressure switch sensing airflow or by a current sensor on the appliance circuit.

The catch is that the fan sits in the lint stream. Lint coats the impeller blades, builds up in the housing, and unbalances the fan. A loaded fan moves less air while using the same power, and because it is hidden in an attic, nothing tells you it has stopped doing its job. Drying times just creep up and get blamed on the dryer.

Signs Yours Needs Attention

How a Tired Booster Fan Shows Itself

If your home has a booster fan and any of these apply, it is worth including in the visit.

🕓

Drying times climbing on a long run

The classic pattern. The duct may be reasonably clean while the fan that makes the length workable is loaded with lint.

🔊

New noise from the attic when the dryer runs

Rattling, grinding, or a rising whine. Usually an unbalanced impeller or a bearing coming to the end of its life.

🚫

The fan does not start with the dryer

If the switch or sensor has failed, the fan is simply an obstruction in the duct rather than a help.

💨

The fan runs but airflow is still poor

Either the fan is loaded, or the duct is restricted somewhere else, or both. Measurement separates them.

🌡️

The fan runs on after the dryer stops

Some models are designed to; many are not. A fan that never switches off is worth looking at.

🧺

Lint visible around the fan housing

Lint escaping at the housing means it is loaded inside and probably leaking at a seam.

⚠️ A booster fan is wired equipment. We clean and inspect; we do not install, rewire or replace the electrical side, and neither should anyone without the right licence.
How We Service One

Confirm It Works, Then Clean What It Has Collected

A booster fan is only ever serviced alongside the duct, because cleaning one without the other tells you nothing.

1

Confirm there is one, and find it

Not every long run has a booster fan, and homeowners often do not know. We locate it during the duct trace, usually in the attic partway along the run.

2

Check that it activates

With the dryer running, we confirm the fan starts and that it stops when the dryer does. A fan that never starts is a different problem from a fan that is dirty.

3

Clean the duct and the fan

The run is cleaned with rotary brush and vacuum, and the accessible fan housing and impeller are cleared of lint so the blades are clean and balanced.

4

Measure and report

Airflow before and after, plus a plain statement of the fan’s condition. If it is worn out rather than dirty, we tell you that it needs an electrician or an HVAC contractor, not another cleaning.

Scope

What Booster Fan Service Covers

The electrical boundary on this one is firm, and we would rather be clear about it now than on site.

  • Locating the booster fan during the duct trace
  • Confirming the fan activates with the dryer and stops with it
  • Cleaning lint from the accessible housing and impeller
  • Cleaning the duct run either side of the fan
  • Airflow reading before and after the work
  • A plain assessment of the fan’s condition, with photos where access allows

What this visit does not cover

  • No electrical work. We do not wire, rewire, or replace switches, sensors, or the fan itself. That is a licensed electrical trade.
  • No fan installation. If a long run would benefit from a booster fan that is not there, we will say so and leave the installation to a qualified contractor.
  • No unsafe attic entry. Low clearance, no decking, or extreme heat mean we work from the ends of the run instead.
  • No performance promises. How much a clean booster fan improves your drying times depends on the whole run, and we will show you measurements rather than estimates.

What changes the price

Access is the main factor. A fan on an attic walkway near the hatch is straightforward. One at the far end of a low-clearance attic over a second floor is a different job.

Booster fan service is normally quoted as an addition to cleaning the run rather than on its own, because servicing the fan without cleaning the duct around it does not get you a useful result.

Questions

Dryer Booster Fan Service FAQ

How do I know if I even have a booster fan? +

Most people do not know, and that is normal. They are usually hidden in an attic partway along a long duct run, and there is rarely anything in the laundry room to indicate one exists. We find them while tracing the duct. If your laundry is interior or on a second floor and the run is long, it is worth asking us to look.

Does a dirty booster fan use more electricity? +

A loaded, unbalanced fan works harder for less airflow, so it is less efficient at the job it is there to do. The bigger cost is usually the drying time, because a fan that has stopped helping turns a workable long run back into a marginal one. We will not quote you a figure on either, because it depends on your run and your usage.

Can you replace my booster fan if it has failed? +

No. It is wired equipment and replacing it is licensed electrical work. We will tell you clearly if we think the fan is finished rather than dirty, so you can get the right trade in instead of paying us to clean something that will not improve.

Should I have a booster fan installed on my long run? +

Possibly, but that is a question for a contractor who can assess the whole system and install it properly. Our advice would start with measuring what the run actually does once it is clean — quite a few long runs perform acceptably when the duct is clear, and a fan added to a clean run is a smaller gain than people expect.

How often should a booster fan be cleaned? +

On the same visit as the duct, which for most long runs means roughly annually. Cleaning the duct and leaving the fan loaded puts a restriction right in the middle of the run you just cleaned, which rather defeats the exercise.

Long Duct Run and Rising Drying Times?

Tell us where your laundry room is and how long you think the run is. If there is a booster fan in there, we will find it.
