Start With the Route

The Duct Rarely Runs Where the Floor Plan Suggests

Much of the housing here predates the era when laundry got its own dedicated, straight-shot exhaust line. Washers and dryers were added later, often into a converted porch, an enclosed carport, a rear addition, or a hallway closet carved out of what used to be a bedroom. The duct then took whatever path was open at the time.

That produces routing you cannot guess from the outside: extra elbows to clear a header, a run that turns and travels along a joist bay before surfacing on a side wall, or several feet of slack flexible pipe coiled behind a machine in a shallow closet. Each bend and every inch of unsupported flex slows the air down and gives lint somewhere to settle.

So we verify before we quote. We run the dryer, find the termination that is actually live, and measure what is getting out. On a house that has been remodeled twice, that step alone often changes what the job needs to be.

Worth Checking in an Older House

Four Routing Patterns We Look For

None of these are unusual here. They are just the marks a house leaves when it has been changed over time.

The Abandoned Stub

When laundry moves, the old exterior hood often stays. We confirm which opening is live so the cleaning goes down the run your dryer is really using.

Slack Flex Behind the Machine

Shallow closets tempt whoever installed the dryer to leave the extra length coiled. Compressed flexible duct is where lint packs first.

Two Units, One Wall

Divided houses and converted spaces can put two terminations within a few feet of each other. We identify each one before anyone assumes.

A Hood That Stopped Moving

Coastal air corrodes hinges and rusts pest screens. A flap that no longer swings freely holds back the whole run, no matter how clean the duct is.

Lake Worth Beach Homeowner Questions

Our laundry was moved into a converted porch. How do you find where the duct actually runs? +

We trace it rather than assume. In a converted porch or enclosed carport, the duct usually takes whatever path was available at the time — through a soffit, along a joist bay, or up a knee wall — and the exterior hood is not always on the nearest wall. We run the dryer, follow the airflow to the live termination, and confirm the route before quoting the work.

We own a divided house where two dryers vent through the same exterior wall. Can you tell which hood belongs to which unit? +

Usually, yes. With one dryer running we check which flap moves and which hood is passing warm air, then repeat for the second unit. It matters in Lake Worth Beach because older divided houses were often converted one unit at a time, so the two runs can be very different lengths. If a hood cannot be reached safely, we tell you what we could and could not confirm.

Our rear addition has a low crawl area and no attic access above the laundry. Can the duct still be reached? +

Often, but not always. We work the run from the dryer connection and from the exterior termination, which reaches most of the pathway in a single-story addition. Where a section is buried behind finished framing with no access point, we clean what is accessible, show you where the limit is, and explain the options instead of opening walls.

Does being this close to the coast affect the exterior vent hood? +

It can. Salt air is hard on thin metal, and we regularly find hoods where the flap has stiffened, the hinge has corroded, or a pest screen has rusted and started catching lint. A flap that no longer swings freely restricts the whole run, so we check its movement on every visit and tell you when a termination looks past its service life.

Dryer Vents in Lake Worth Beach Housing

Lake Worth Beach has some of the oldest housing we work in, and almost none of it was designed around a clothes dryer. In the historic districts, laundry was fitted into whatever room could take it long after the house went up — and in a building with plaster walls, original framing and no obvious route to an exterior face, the duct went wherever it could. We trace the actual run before quoting, because what is behind the machine rarely matches what anyone expects.

Two things come up repeatedly here. The first is excessive length: a run that wanders to find a wall it can exit through, with each extra foot and each extra turn giving lint somewhere to settle. The second is flexible foil, which sags between supports and crushes easily — in those cases replacing the reachable sections with metal does more than another cleaning will. On the small multi-unit buildings, access to the termination sometimes has to come through the owner rather than the tenant.

Every dryer vent service we offer in Lake Worth Beach

  • Dryer vent cleaning — rotary brush and vacuum through the accessible pathway, airflow measured before and after.
  • Clogged dryer vent diagnosis — we trace the run and find where the air actually stops before anything is cleaned.
  • Blockage removal — for a run that is packed rather than dirty: compacted lint, a collapsed section, nesting material.
  • Roof dryer vent cleaning — long attic runs and roof caps, cleaned from the laundry side and cleared at the cap where access is safe.

  • Dryer vent airflow testing — a free measured reading and a written report, with no obligation to book anything.
