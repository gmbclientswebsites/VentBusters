Clogged Dryer Vent

A clog announces itself in the laundry room long before anyone looks at the duct. We trace the run, find where the air actually stops, clear what we can reach, and show you the airflow reading before and after so the result is not a matter of opinion.

What Is Actually Happening

A Clog Is Lint Winning a Slow Argument With Airflow

Every load pushes hot, damp air out through the duct, and the lint filter never catches all of it. What escapes rides the airstream until the air slows down — at an elbow, where a flexible connector sags behind the machine, or at the termination flap where the duct meets the outdoors. There the lint settles and stays.

The buildup is self-accelerating. A narrower duct moves less air, less air means lint drops out sooner, and the restriction grows from both ends. That is why a vent can run acceptably for years and then seem to fail in the space of a month: the last stretch of the curve is steep.

In South Florida the damp air makes it worse. Lint that would stay loose and dusty in a dry climate mats together in humidity, so what we pull out of a Palm Beach County duct is more often a felted plug than a drift of fluff.

Usual Cause
Lint accumulating at bends and the termination
Typical Visit
About 30–45 minutes
Diagnosis
Airflow measured before and after
Escalates To
Blockage removal if the run is packed
Symptoms

Six Signs You Are Looking at a Clog

Any one of these on its own is worth a look. Two or more together, and the duct is almost always the reason.

🕓

Two cycles to dry one load

The single most reliable sign. Damp air that cannot leave the drum has to be reheated, so the machine runs and runs without finishing the job.

🌡️

The laundry room gets hot

Heat and moisture that should be going outside are staying inside instead, usually because they are leaking back out of a restricted duct.

💨

Little or no air at the outside hood

Hold a hand near the termination with the dryer running. Strong, steady air is normal. A weak trickle, or a flap that never lifts, is not.

🧺

Lint around the hood or behind the dryer

Lint escaping at the ends means the pathway between them is too tight for it to pass through cleanly.

🔥

A burning or scorched smell

Stop using the dryer. This warrants having both the appliance and the vent looked at before the next load.

⚠️

The dryer shuts off mid-cycle

Many machines have a thermal cut-off that trips when exhaust cannot escape. Repeated shutdowns point at the duct, not the dryer.

⚠️ If you smell burning while the dryer runs, stop using it and have the appliance and the vent evaluated before the next load. If there is smoke or fire, leave the home and call 911.
How We Clear It

Find the Restriction First, Then Clear It

Clearing a vent without locating the restriction is guesswork. The order below is the same on every visit.

1

Trace the run

We follow the duct from the dryer to the termination — wall, roof, or soffit — and note the length, the number of turns, and the material. A run we have not mapped is a run we cannot honestly quote.

2

Measure the baseline

With the dryer running, we take an airflow reading at the termination before touching anything. That number is what the after reading gets compared against.

3

Clear the accessible pathway

A flexible rotary brush is driven through the duct while a vacuum captures what it frees, so the lint leaves the system instead of moving further down it.

4

Re-measure and show you

We take the airflow reading again, reconnect the dryer, and walk you through what came out, with photos when conditions allow.

Scope

What a Clog Visit Covers

A clog that cleans out and a run that is packed solid are two different jobs. We tell you which one you have before any extra work starts.

  • Tracing and mapping the duct run end to end
  • Airflow reading before and after the work
  • Rotary brush and vacuum cleaning of the accessible pathway
  • Flexible transition connector cleaned and reseated
  • Exterior termination cleared and the flap checked, where it can be reached safely
  • A plain explanation of what we found, with photos when conditions allow

What this visit does not cover

  • No drywall opening. We do not cut into walls, ceilings, or floors to reach buried duct.
  • No appliance repair. No heating elements, drums, belts, or control boards, and no disconnecting gas lines.
  • No unsafe roof work. A roof termination is reached only when it can be done safely.
  • Heavy obstructions are quoted separately. A packed run or a nest at the hood is blockage removal, priced after we show you what is in there.

What changes the price

Length and complexity of the run do most of the work on the number. A short wall vent behind a garage dryer is the simple case. A second-floor laundry venting through an attic to a roof cap is longer, has more turns, and needs roof access.

The other factor is what we find. If the duct is crushed, separated, or the hose behind the machine has collapsed, cleaning alone will not fix the airflow — that becomes repair or replacement, quoted on its own so you can decide.

Clogged Dryer Vent FAQ

How do I know it is the vent and not the dryer? +

Airflow at the termination is the quickest test. Run the dryer and hold a hand near the outside hood: if the air is weak or the flap never lifts, the duct is restricting it. If airflow outside is strong but clothes still will not dry, the problem is more likely in the appliance, and an appliance technician is the right call rather than us.

Can I just clear it myself with a brush kit from the hardware store? +

Sometimes, on a short straight run you can reach from both ends. The kits struggle on long runs, on anything with several turns, and on roof terminations, and a brush pushed without a vacuum tends to compact the lint ahead of it rather than remove it. If you have already tried and airflow did not improve, the restriction is usually further in than the brush reached.

How quickly can someone come out? +

Same-day and next-day appointments are often available. Call (561) 884-4648 and we will tell you honestly what the schedule looks like rather than booking a window we cannot hold.

Will clearing the clog fix my drying times? +

If a restricted duct is what was holding the moisture in, drying times usually improve, and the airflow reading will show you how much changed. What we will not do is promise a number in advance — a worn heating element or a failing moisture sensor produces the same complaint, and cleaning the duct does not touch either one.

How often does this come back? +

It depends on loads per week, the length of the run, and whether anyone in the house dries pet bedding or heavy fleece. Many South Florida homes land somewhere around once a year. We would rather tell you what your airflow looks like at the next visit than sell you a schedule you may not need.
