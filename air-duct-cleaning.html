The Service

What We Clean, and How Far We Can Reach

Air duct cleaning here means mechanically cleaning the accessible parts of your HVAC distribution system: the supply runs that push conditioned air into your rooms, the return runs that pull it back, and the registers and grilles at either end. Where the system design allows, that also covers accessible plenum and trunk-line surfaces.

Not every duct in a Florida home is reachable. Flex duct buried in a tight attic run, sections sealed inside a wall or ceiling chase, and anything that would have to be cut open sit outside this service. We flag those limits during the evaluation rather than implying the whole system was cleaned when it wasn't.

Honest Guidance

What the EPA Says About Duct Cleaning

We would rather point you at the guidance than talk around it.

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency does not recommend that air ducts be cleaned on a routine, fixed schedule — only as needed. The EPA also states that duct cleaning has never been shown to prevent health problems.

It does describe conditions that make cleaning worth considering: visible mold growth on hard surfaces inside the ducts or on other system components, an infestation of vermin such as rodents or insects, or ducts so clogged with debris that particles are actually being released into the room through the supply registers.

That is the standard we work to. If none of those conditions are present in your system, we will tell you duct cleaning is not something you need right now — a shorter visit than a sales pitch, and the honest answer.

Scope Limits

What This Service Does Not Cover

Clear boundaries beat vague promises. Here is what we will not do on your job.

✕

No mold work of any kind

We do not identify, test, sample or treat mold, and we will not tell you whether what you are looking at is mold. If we find growth on system components, we stop and refer you to an appropriately licensed mold assessor or remediator.

✕

No calendar-based upselling

We do not put homes on a fixed cleaning cycle, because routine scheduled cleaning is not what the EPA recommends. We look at the condition of your system, whether or not that leads to a job.

✕

No claims about what you breathe

Duct cleaning has not been shown to prevent health problems, so we do not market it that way. We report what we removed and the condition of the accessible components — nothing beyond that.

Questions

Air Duct Cleaning FAQ

How do I know if my air ducts actually need cleaning? +

The EPA points to three situations worth acting on: visible mold growth inside the ducts or on other system components, an infestation of vermin such as rodents or insects, or ducts so clogged with debris that particles are actually being released through the supply registers. We evaluate your system against that standard.

Does duct cleaning prevent health problems? +

No, and we will not tell you otherwise. The EPA states that duct cleaning has never been shown to prevent health problems. What we can report is what we found in the accessible ductwork, what we removed, and the condition of the components when we finished.

How often should air ducts be cleaned? +

There is no schedule we put you on. The EPA does not recommend routine cleaning at fixed intervals, only cleaning as needed. Frequency depends on the condition of your system, so we evaluate it and describe what we see instead of selling you a calendar.

Do you test for or remove mold? +

No. We do not identify, test, sample or remediate mold. If we find growth on system components, we stop, show you what we are looking at, and recommend you contact an appropriately licensed mold assessor or remediator before any further work.

How is the scope of my job decided? +

We evaluate the system first, then walk you through which supply runs, return runs, registers and grilles are accessible. Scope is explained before work begins, and anything we cannot reach is recorded on the written summary.
