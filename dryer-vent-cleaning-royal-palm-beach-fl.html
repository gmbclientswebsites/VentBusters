Floor Plans, Not Guesswork

What a Garage-Adjacent Laundry Room Does to the Run

When the washer and dryer share a wall with the garage, the duct rarely travels far — but it almost never travels straight. It leaves the back of the appliance, turns hard into the wall cavity, and then makes another turn to reach a hood on the side or rear elevation. Every elbow adds resistance the same way extra feet of pipe do, which is why a run that measures short on paper can still behave like a long one.

That is also why the transition behind the dryer matters so much in these homes. Push the appliance back tight against a stub in a shallow laundry closet and the flexible connector kinks. Lint then collects at the crush point rather than leaving the run, and the first symptom is usually drying cycles that need a second round.

Routing Patterns

Three Ways These Ducts Usually Travel

We read the exterior first. Where the hood sits tells us most of what happens behind the drywall.

🧱

Short Lateral to a Sidewall

The most forgiving version: a brief run inside the shared wall to a hood a few feet away. Cleaning is usually a straightforward brush-and-vacuum pass from both ends.

🚗

Across the Garage Ceiling

The duct climbs into the garage ceiling cavity and crosses to a hood on the far elevation. Longer, with more joints — and the section above the garage is the part homeowners never see.

🏠

Up Through a Two-Story

In two-story models the run can climb a chase and terminate high on a wall or on the roof. We work these from a ladder or the roof when conditions allow, and reschedule when they do not.

Answers for This Kind of House

Where does the dryer vent exit in a Royal Palm Beach home with the laundry room beside the garage? +

In most of these floor plans the duct leaves the back of the dryer, turns into the wall shared with the garage, and either crosses the garage ceiling cavity or runs a short lateral to a hood on the side or rear elevation. We locate the termination from outside first, because the hood tells us the direction of the run and roughly how many turns are between it and the appliance.

Do I need HOA approval before you work on the exterior vent hood in my Royal Palm Beach subdivision? +

Cleaning itself does not change the appearance of the elevation, so it is not usually treated as an architectural change. Replacing a damaged hood with a different style or color can be, depending on your association's rules. If a hood needs to be swapped we will tell you what we recommend and let you clear it with your HOA before we order anything.

Our dryer is in the garage. Does that change how the vent gets cleaned? +
Is duct cleaning worth it if my Royal Palm Beach house is a newer build? +

Newer does not mean untouched. Construction dust, drywall fines and insulation fibers can settle in flex duct during the build and stay there, and humid Florida air moves through that same duct every day afterward. An inspection is the honest answer: we look at the return, the plenum and a few boots, show you what is there, and tell you if cleaning is worth doing or not.

Dryer Vent Service in Royal Palm Beach

Royal Palm Beach is almost entirely single-family and almost entirely suburban, built out over about three decades, and the vintage of the house tells you most of what you need to know about the vent. The earlier subdivisions put the laundry in the garage or against an exterior wall, which gives a short, straightforward run that a standard cleaning handles in well under an hour.

The later ones moved it indoors, and from there the exhaust has to climb. Those runs are longer, turn more, and are where we find the obstructions that have gone past dirty into packed — compacted lint at an elbow, or nesting material where a roof cap has lost its flap. That is blockage removal, quoted after we have shown you what is actually in there rather than before. Either way, we measure airflow before and after, so the result is a number rather than an assurance.

Every dryer vent service we offer in Royal Palm Beach

  • Dryer vent cleaning — rotary brush and vacuum through the accessible pathway, airflow measured before and after.
  • Clogged dryer vent diagnosis — we trace the run and find where the air actually stops before anything is cleaned.
  • Blockage removal — for a run that is packed rather than dirty: compacted lint, a collapsed section, nesting material.
  • Roof dryer vent cleaning — long attic runs and roof caps, cleaned from the laundry side and cleared at the cap where access is safe.
  • Dryer vent repair — crushed pipe, separated joints, leaking seams, a damaged hood or a flap that will not swing.
  • Dryer vent replacement — rigid or semi-rigid metal where foil, vinyl or repeatedly crushed duct is past patching.
  • Dryer vent reconnection — duct that has come apart in an attic or wall and is venting into the structure.
  • Transition hose replacement — the short connector behind the machine, cut to length and clamped rather than taped.
  • Booster fan service — inspection and cleaning of an in-line fan on a long run. No electrical work.
  • Dryer vent airflow testing — a free measured reading and a written report, with no obligation to book anything.
