Why It Matters

Why Dryer Vent Cleaning Matters

A dryer vent is the one duct in your house that fills itself up on purpose. Every load sends lint into it, and the consequences arrive in this order.

🕓

Clothes take two cycles to dry

The signature symptom. Damp air that cannot leave the drum has to be reheated, so the machine runs and runs without finishing the load.

🔥

Lint is fuel in a hot place

Failure to clean was the leading factor in home clothes-dryer fires analysed by the U.S. Fire Administration. A restricted duct means more lint and more heat in the same space.

🌡️

Heat ends up in your laundry room

Warmth and moisture that should be leaving the house are staying in it, which in a closed interior laundry is impossible to miss.

⚡

Every cycle costs more than it should

A dryer running twice as long to do the same work is using the energy twice. How much that is worth depends on your rates and your laundry volume.

🧺

Buildup accelerates once it starts

A narrower duct moves less air, less air drops lint sooner, and the restriction grows from both ends. That is why a vent seems fine for years and then fails in a month.

💨

Poor airflow hides other faults

A crushed section, a separated joint and a plug of lint all look identical from the laundry room. Measuring airflow and tracing the run is how you tell them apart.

If any of those sound like your laundry room, start with clogged dryer vent — or have the airflow tested and find out where you stand before spending anything.

How It Works

Inspect, Clean, Verify

Three steps, in the same order on every job, so nothing gets skipped when a visit runs long.

1

Inspect

We trace the duct from the dryer to the termination, note the length, turns and material, and take an airflow reading before anything is touched. A run we have not mapped is a run we cannot honestly quote.

2

Clean

A flexible rotary brush is driven through the duct while a vacuum captures what it frees, so the lint leaves the system instead of relocating further down it. The connector behind the machine is cleaned and reseated.

3

Verify

We measure airflow again, reconnect the dryer, and walk you through what came out, with before-and-after photos when conditions allow. If the number did not move, we tell you that rather than packing up.

Dryer Vent FAQ

How much does dryer vent cleaning cost? +

It depends on the length and routing of your run and how much buildup is in it. A short wall vent behind a garage dryer and a second-floor laundry venting through an attic to a roof cap are genuinely different jobs, so we quote after we know which one you have. Call (561) 884-4648, describe your laundry setup, and we will give you a realistic range before anyone is dispatched.

How often should a dryer vent be cleaned in Florida? +

Many South Florida homes land somewhere around once a year, but usage matters more than the calendar. A household running several loads a day, a long run with multiple turns, or anyone drying pet bedding and heavy fleece will need it sooner. The airflow reading we take at each visit is a better guide than a fixed interval, and we will tell you what yours looked like.

How do I know if my dryer vent is clogged? +

The most reliable sign is clothes needing two cycles to dry. After that: a laundry room that gets hot, little or no air at the outside hood while the dryer runs, lint collecting around the hood or behind the machine, and a dryer that shuts off before the load is finished. Any one of those is worth checking; two together and it is almost always the duct.

Can a clogged dryer vent cause a fire? +

Failure to clean was the leading factor in home clothes-dryer fires analysed by the U.S. Fire Administration. Lint is combustible, and a restricted duct makes the appliance run hotter than it was designed to. Cleaning the vent removes that fuel and restores the airflow; what we will not tell you is that any service prevents fires, because no service can make that promise honestly.

How long does a dryer vent cleaning take? +

About 30 to 45 minutes for a typical run. Long ducts, several bends, a roof termination, or heavy buildup can push the visit past an hour. If we expect that, we tell you on site before we start rather than after.

Do you clean air ducts or HVAC systems? +

No. We used to, and we stopped. Dryer vents are what we specialise in now, and we would rather tell you plainly that air duct work is not us than take the job and do it adequately. If that is what you need, an HVAC contractor is the right call.

What if my laundry room is upstairs or in the middle of the house? +

Then your dryer almost certainly vents up through the attic and out of a cap on the roof. Those runs are longer, have more turns, and clog faster than a wall vent. We clean them from the laundry side with vacuum collection and clear the roof cap where access is genuinely safe. Where it is not safe, we say so and note what we could not inspect.

Do you repair dryer vents as well as clean them? +

Yes, and the distinction matters. Cleaning removes what is inside the duct; repair fixes the duct itself. A crushed section, a joint that pulled apart in the attic, or a termination flap that will not swing will hold your airflow down no matter how clean the pipe is. We show you the fault in photos, then quote the fix separately.

Which areas do you serve? +

Twenty communities from Boca Raton north to Port St. Lucie, including Boynton Beach, Delray Beach, Lake Worth Beach, Wellington, West Palm Beach, Palm Beach Gardens and Jupiter. If you are just outside that map, call and ask rather than assuming; we will give you a straight answer.

Are you insured, and are you licensed? +

We carry insurance. We are not going to describe ourselves as licensed or certified, because dryer vent cleaning is not a licensed trade in Florida and claiming otherwise would be misleading. What we will not do is work outside our scope: no electrical, no gas connections, no HVAC, no mould assessment, and nothing that needs a licence we do not hold.
