HVAC UV Light Systems

A UV system is a lamp mounted inside compatible HVAC equipment, aimed at specific surfaces — typically the cooling coil and drain pan area. It treats the equipment, not the whole house. We review your system, supply a unit that suits it, and arrange installation to the manufacturer's requirements.

Straight Answer First

What a UV System Is, and What It Isn't

Ultraviolet lamps used in residential HVAC are designed to act on the surfaces they can reach with direct light. In most homes that means the face of the evaporator coil and the drain pan below it — the damp, dark parts of an air handler where growth tends to settle in a Florida climate.

How much a given system does depends on the product, where the lamp sits relative to the coil, airflow, how many hours the blower runs, and whether the lamp is maintained. Two homes with the same lamp can see different outcomes.

A UV system is not a cleaning service and it does not sterilize a home. We make no health claims for it — if a company tells you a lamp will fix allergies, treat that as marketing.

Compatibility

Why Equipment, Placement and Lamp Life Decide the Outcome

Not every air handler can take a UV lamp. The cabinet needs room for the lamp and its housing, the coil has to be reachable by direct light, and there has to be a safe way to power and mount the unit without blocking service access. Some closet and attic installations here are simply too tight, and we would rather say so at the quote stage.

Placement matters as much as the product. A lamp set too far from the coil, or aimed at the wrong surface, is not doing the job it was designed for, so we follow the manufacturer's instructions for the system supplied.

Lamps also lose output well before they stop lighting up. Replacement intervals are set by the manufacturer and vary by lamp type and model, so the schedule follows rated service life, not whether the bulb still glows. We hand over the documentation so the interval is on paper.

UV works alongside cleaning, never instead of it. A lamp cannot reach debris deeper in the system, so we usually pair the conversation with air duct cleaning for the accessible components and, where it makes sense, HVAC system sanitization applied per the product label after cleaning. Laundry airflow is a separate job — see dryer vent cleaning. We quote UV systems across our service areas from Boca Raton to Port St. Lucie.

Questions

UV Light System FAQ

Does a UV light replace duct or coil cleaning? +

No. A UV system treats surfaces it can reach with direct light; it does not remove debris that is already there. Clean the accessible components first, then decide whether a lamp is worth adding.

Will a UV system clean the air throughout my home? +

No. A lamp mounted in an air handler acts on the equipment surfaces near it and the air passing directly in front of it. It does not sterilize a home, and we make no health claims for it.

How do I know if my equipment can take a UV system? +

It depends on cabinet space, coil access, mounting points and available power at the air handler. We look at your equipment first, and if it is not a sensible fit we will say so.

How often do UV lamps need to be replaced? +

On the interval the manufacturer specifies for that model. Lamps commonly keep glowing after their rated output has dropped, so replacement follows the manufacturer's stated service life, not whether the bulb still lights.

Can a UV system deal with mold in my system? +

We do not perform mold assessment or mold remediation, and a UV lamp should not be treated as a fix for a suspected mold problem. If you are concerned, speak with a qualified mold professional in Florida.
