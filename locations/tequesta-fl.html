What We See Here

The Hood Behind the Hedge

In neighborhoods this settled, the shrubs planted along the foundation for privacy are now taller than whoever planted them. Sidewall dryer terminations sit low, often at knee height, and a hedge grown flush to the wall leaves the damper flap nothing to open into. The flap stays half-shut, exhaust backs up, and lint packs into the last few feet of duct where nobody can see it.

Shade compounds it. A hood under heavy canopy stays damp between rains, and damp lint sticks to the flap instead of blowing clear. Those same sheltered hoods are the ones we most often find with nesting material or a wasp nest tucked behind the louvers.

So the first thing our technician does on a Tequesta property is walk the exterior and part the plantings. Everything else waits until we can see the termination.

On the Day

How a Grown-In Property Gets Worked

1

Locate the live termination

We trace the run from the appliance outward. Remodeled homes sometimes keep a capped hood from an old laundry location beside the live one.

2

Clear the hood first

Debris, nesting material and matted lint come out of the cap and damper first, so nothing gets pushed back down the run.

3

Brush and vacuum the run

Rotary brushing works the accessible duct while a vacuum captures what breaks loose at the appliance end, elbows included.

4

Confirm airflow and flap

We re-check exhaust at the hood, watch the damper swing fully, and tell you how much clearance the landscaping is leaving it.

Five Services, Scheduled Around One Visit

Most Tequesta calls start with dryer vent cleaning — a full clean of the accessible pathway from the appliance connection out to the hood. When drying times have already doubled or the dryer shuts itself off mid-cycle, that is usually a job for dryer vent blockage removal, where the obstruction is located and extracted rather than simply brushed past.

On the HVAC side, air duct cleaning covers the accessible supply and return components. In houses added onto over the years, that ductwork is often a mix of original and newer runs, and we work only what can be safely reached. For an ongoing measure inside the air handler, ask about HVAC UV light systems — we scope those conservatively, to the manufacturer's stated coverage and no further.

Finally, HVAC system sanitization is applied per the product label after cleaning is finished. It is a treatment step, not mold assessment or remediation; if you suspect a moisture problem behind a wall or in the air handler closet, that calls for a specialist in that field.

Not satisfied? Tell us within 14 days and we'll re-clean the accessible components at no charge, or refund that service. Tequesta is one of the communities on our full service area map, from Boca Raton up through Port St. Lucie.

Questions About Older Homes and Grown-In Lots

The dryer vent hood on our Tequesta house is buried behind hedges. Do we need to cut the landscaping back first? +

No. Our technicians work around foundation plantings by hand to reach the hood. If a shrub has grown so tightly against the wall that the damper flap cannot swing open, we will show you what is in the way and how much clearance the termination needs, so you can decide what to trim on your own schedule.

Our vent terminates on the roof under heavy tree canopy. Can that still be cleaned? +

Usually, when conditions allow. A roof cap sitting under a mature canopy collects leaf litter and twigs on top of ordinary lint, so we plan for debris at the cap as well as inside the duct. Safe roof access depends on pitch, height and weather, and we will tell you before we start if the roof cannot be worked safely that day.

Our older Tequesta home was remodeled and the laundry moved to an interior wall. Does that make the duct harder to clean? +

It generally makes the run longer and adds elbows, and elbows are exactly where lint collects. Relocated laundry rooms often route through a wall cavity or the attic before reaching an exterior wall. We confirm the live exit point before brushing anything, because remodels sometimes leave an old capped hood on the wall right beside the one still in use.

Does the shade in older Tequesta neighborhoods change how often vents need attention? +

It can. A shaded exterior wall stays damp longer, and damp lint clings to the hood and damper instead of blowing clear. Shaded hoods are also more inviting to nesting birds and insects. Many single-family homes here do well with a yearly look, while a household running several loads a day may want it checked sooner.
