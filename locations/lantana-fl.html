How Lantana Homes Are Built

A Short Duct Run Is Not the Same as a Clean One

Most houses in town were built when the laundry sat against an exterior wall, so the duct from the dryer to the outside is frequently only six to twelve feet. Homeowners read that as a reason to skip cleaning. In practice the opposite holds: a short run ends in a wall cap or hood sitting low, close to landscaping and fully exposed to weather. Lint packs into that last foot of duct and behind the flapper door, then the run backs up from the outside in.

We work from the termination inward, brushing and vacuuming the accessible pathway rather than pushing debris deeper into the wall. Where a hood is damaged, painted shut or screened over — a common find on older exteriors here — we show you photos of what we found and what changed.

The Part Facing the Weather

What Coastal Exposure Does to a Vent Termination

Salt-laden air reaches most Lantana neighborhoods. On vent hardware that shows up as corroded fasteners, stiff or seized flapper doors, and thin metal caps that no longer close cleanly.

  • Flapper doors that no longer swing freely
  • Corroded, loose or missing exterior wall caps
  • Pest screens added over a hood, which trap lint
  • Crushed or kinked transition hose behind the dryer
  • Separated joints in reachable sections of duct
  • Airflow at the hood checked before and after

Local Questions

Lantana Homeowner Questions

Our Lantana condo association maintains the building exterior — can we still have our dryer vent cleaned? +

Usually yes. In most low-rise buildings the duct running from your dryer to the outside wall belongs to the unit, while the hood on the exterior is a shared surface. We clean the accessible run from inside the unit and photograph the termination. If ladder access to a second- or third-floor hood is needed, ask your association or property manager to approve it first and we will schedule around their answer.

Does coastal salt air in Lantana damage dryer vent hoods? +

It can. Salt-laden air speeds up corrosion on fasteners, hinges and thin metal caps, and a flapper door that no longer swings freely will hold lint at the exact point where the duct leaves the wall. We check the flapper and the hood on every visit and tell you plainly if the hardware is past cleaning and should be replaced.

My Lantana house is older and the dryer run is only a few feet long. Is cleaning still worth it? +

Short runs still clog, and they clog at the end you cannot see. A six- to ten-foot run through an exterior wall collects lint at the wall cap, behind the flapper and in the final elbow, and in older homes that section is often buried in a wall cavity or hidden behind the washer. Length is a poor indicator of condition. Airflow at the hood is a much better one.

Can you clean the ducts in an older Lantana home with the air handler in a closet? +

Yes. Compact older houses here commonly place the air handler in a hallway closet or the garage, with short trunk lines and one large return. We clean the accessible components, including supply boots, registers, the return and reachable duct sections, and we tell you up front if any part of the system is sealed, buried in a slab or otherwise not accessible.
