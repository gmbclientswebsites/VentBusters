How the Duct Actually Runs

Vertical Buildings Mean Vertical Ductwork

In a single-family house the dryer duct usually makes a short, mostly straight trip to an exterior wall. In a Highland Beach tower it rarely does. The duct leaves a shallow laundry closet, turns almost immediately into a wall or ceiling cavity, and joins a chase that carries exhaust upward through the building toward a roof termination. Some stacks include a booster fan somewhere in that path.

Two things follow from that geometry. First, there is simply more interior surface for lint to settle on than a townhouse run would have. Second, the tightest elbow is almost always within a foot or two of the machine, which is where compaction tends to begin and where a restriction shows up first as longer drying cycles and a warm, humid closet.

That is also why we measure airflow at the connection before and after we work. On a vertical run you cannot judge the result by what comes out of the hose alone.

Working Inside a Building, Not Just a Unit

The Order We Do Things in a Tower

A condo appointment has a step before the vent work that a house never needs: getting the technician, the equipment and the paperwork upstairs without disrupting the building.

1

Building Requirements First

We ask who manages the property and what they require — insurance documentation on file, a vendor sign-in, or a reserved freight elevator window. Sorting that out at booking avoids a wasted trip.

2

Protect the Closet

Stacked units sit in tight bi-fold closets with finished flooring right up to the base. We lay protection down, ease the appliance out only as far as the connections allow, and keep the work area contained.

3

Clean the Accessible Run

Brushes, rods and vacuum equipment are matched to the duct we can actually reach — from the transition at the machine to the point where the run disappears into the common chase.

4

Reconnect and Verify

The transition is reseated properly rather than crushed back into place, airflow is rechecked, and we tell you plainly what we could and could not see beyond your unit.

Coverage for Highland Beach Residences

Five Services, Fitted to a Condo Mechanical Closet

  • Dryer vent cleaning for stacked laundry closets — the accessible run from the machine connection outward, with airflow checked either side of the work.
  • Blockage removal where the run turns vertical — for compacted lint at the first elbow or at the transition into the chase.
  • Air duct cleaning for condo air handlers — the short supply and return runs feeding a closet-mounted unit, cleaned at accessible components.
  • HVAC UV light systems where clearance allows — fitted inside the air handler when there is room; performance depends on the equipment and the manufacturer's specifications.
  • HVAC system sanitization applied after cleaning — used per product label. It is not mold assessment or remediation.

Condo Questions

What Highland Beach Owners Ask Us

My Highland Beach condo has a stacked washer and dryer in a narrow closet. Can the vent still be cleaned? +

Usually, yes. Most stacked units can be eased forward far enough to reach the transition connection behind them, and we protect the flooring and surrounding cabinetry while we work. If the clearance is too tight to disconnect anything safely, we stop and tell you what we found rather than force it.

The duct from my unit joins a shared vertical chase. Which part is mine to maintain? +

The run inside your unit, up to the point where it enters the common chase, is normally the owner side of that line. The shared riser, any booster fan in it, and the roof termination are usually building property handled by the association. We clean the accessible portion on your side and report what we could observe beyond it.

Does my building need a certificate of insurance or a reserved service elevator before you arrive? +

Many Highland Beach buildings ask for both. Send us the management office contact when you book and we will forward our paperwork in advance and take an appointment inside the window your building allows. We schedule Monday through Thursday 7am to 6pm and Friday 7am to 3pm.

Does salt air on an oceanfront elevation affect the exterior vent hood? +

It can. Hoods, flaps, and dampers facing the ocean sit in constant salt spray, and a flap that has corroded partly open or partly shut may restrict airflow even when the duct behind it is clear. Where the termination is reachable we note its condition; on upper floors that hardware is often serviceable only from the building side.
