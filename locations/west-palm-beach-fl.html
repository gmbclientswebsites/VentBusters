Dryer Vent & Air Duct Cleaning in West Palm Beach, FL

No other city in the county packs this much variety into one set of limits: towers on the Intracoastal, 1920s cottages a few blocks inland, and subdivisions built decades later out west. The duct behind the dryer looks different in every one of them, so we plan the visit around the building rather than a script.

Housing Stock

One City, Three Very Different Vent Runs

Where a home sits in West Palm Beach tells us more about the duct than the square footage does.

🏙️

Downtown and waterfront towers

In a high-rise the dryer almost never vents to a wall a few steps away. Ducting is routed through a soffit or chase toward a shared shaft, so the accessible portion ends where the unit does. Building rules decide the rest.

🏡

El Cid, Flamingo Park and the older inland blocks

Homes from the 1920s and 30s were not drawn with a laundry room. The dryer landed wherever a later owner had space, and the duct followed whatever cavity was open — which is why two neighboring houses can route in opposite directions.

🧭

The newer western subdivisions

Two-story plans push laundry upstairs. That usually means a longer run, more turns and a termination on the roof or a high gable instead of a wall cap you can inspect from the driveway.

How We Work Here

Routing Decides the Visit, Not the Zip Code

A short straight run behind a ground-floor wall and a thirty-foot upstairs run with four elbows are not the same job, even a few miles apart. We trace where the duct actually goes, check the transition hose behind the appliance, and look at the exterior termination — the point that quietly does most of the restricting.

Proximity to the water matters too. Salt air works on exterior hoods and damper flaps, and a pest screen packed with lint can hold air back long after the run itself is clear. We clean the accessible components, then show you what we found.

Local Questions

What West Palm Beach Homeowners Ask

I'm in a downtown West Palm Beach high-rise — is the dryer vent mine or the association's? +

It varies by building. In most towers the ducting inside your unit is the owner's and the shared shaft belongs to the association. We clean the accessible run from the dryer to where it leaves the unit, and we tell you plainly if the restriction appears to sit past that point. Check your association's rules first, since some buildings require notice or use their own vendor for shared shafts.

Our El Cid house was built long before anyone planned a laundry room. Can the vent still be cleaned? +

Usually. In El Cid and Flamingo Park the dryer often ended up on a converted porch, in a garage corner or inside a hall closet, with the duct threaded through whatever cavity was available at the time. We work the accessible run from both ends where we can reach them and we do not open walls or plaster. If a section sits behind finished construction, we show you where it is instead of guessing.

Our two-story home in the western suburbs vents through the roof. Does that change the visit? +

It can. Second-floor laundry rooms in the newer western subdivisions usually mean a longer run with more turns and a termination on the roof rather than a wall cap you can reach from the ground. We work the run from the appliance side and reach the termination when roof pitch, height and weather allow. If safe access is not possible that day, we say so rather than force it.

We live near the Intracoastal and the exterior vent hood looks corroded. Does that matter? +

It can matter. Salt-laden air is hard on exterior hardware, and a hood with seized damper flaps or a lint-packed pest screen may restrict airflow even after the duct behind it is clear. We clean the accessible components and point out corrosion, stuck flaps or screening that could be holding air back, so you can decide what to do about the hood itself.
