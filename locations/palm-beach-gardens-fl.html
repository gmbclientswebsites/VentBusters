Local Housing Stock

Newer Construction, Longer Duct Runs

Much of the housing in Palm Beach Gardens went up as part of large master-planned communities, and those floor plans share a few habits: interior laundry rooms, second-floor bonus space, and a garage sitting on the opposite side of the house from the utility closet. That layout is comfortable to live in. It is also why a dryer duct here often runs farther, and turns more corners, than the same appliance would need in a smaller older house.

Distance matters because lint settles wherever air slows down. Every elbow, every transition from flex to rigid pipe and every foot of horizontal travel gives damp lint another chance to stick. Newer construction is not exempt — it simply hides the buildup better, because the run is buried in a ceiling chase or a soffit instead of poking out of a nearby wall.

When a dryer needs two cycles to finish a normal load, that is usually restriction rather than a failing appliance. Dryer vent cleaning clears the accessible pathway end to end; when something is genuinely packed or a bird has nested in the termination, dryer vent blockage removal is the heavier version of that same job.

Two Systems, One Visit

What Multi-Zone Equipment Changes

Two air handlers means two of nearly everything: separate returns, separate supply trunks, two blower compartments, two coils sitting over two drain pans. A visit that only addresses the downstairs equipment leaves half the house untouched — and through a Gardens summer, the upstairs zone is usually the one running hardest.

So we count the system before we price it: what is accessible in the attic, closet or garage, how many returns feed each handler, how far each trunk travels. Zoning dampers and long branch runs change the time on site, and you should hear that up front.

Questions From Gardens Homeowners

Answers Before You Book

We have two air handlers and separate upstairs and downstairs zones. Is that one job or two? +

It is one visit, but it is scoped as two systems. Each handler has its own returns, its own supply trunk and its own blower compartment, so we count and inspect both before quoting. Homes in Palm Beach Gardens frequently run this way, and a quote built around a single-zone assumption will not match what is actually in your attic.

Our community is guard-gated and vendors have to register. How do you handle access? +

Give us the community name and the gate procedure when you book. Most Palm Beach Gardens associations want the vendor added to a visitor list or called in by the resident ahead of time, and handling that before the appointment window keeps the technician from sitting at the callbox.

The laundry room sits in the middle of our floor plan, so the dryer duct runs a long way. Does that change the cleaning? +

Yes. A long interior run has more elbows and more horizontal travel, and lint settles wherever the air slows down. Those runs take longer to clear and often need to be worked from both the appliance side and the termination. We check the termination as well, since a run that ends on a roof or a high wall behaves differently than one exiting at ground level.

Our home is newer construction. Is duct cleaning worth doing this soon? +

Age of the house is less useful than what the system looks like inside. Construction dust, drywall fines and insulation fibers can sit in supply trunks from the build itself, and a newer home simply hides buildup better because the runs are tucked into chases and soffits. We inspect the accessible components first and tell you honestly if cleaning can wait.
