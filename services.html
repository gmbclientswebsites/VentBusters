Dryer Vent Services

Dryer vents are not a sideline for us — they are the whole business. Ten services covering everything from a routine cleaning to a duct that has come apart in your attic, for homes, condos and communities across Palm Beach County and the Treasure Coast.

Our dryer vent services

Dryer Vent Cleaning

The core service. Rotary brush and vacuum cleaning of the accessible exhaust pathway, with airflow measured before and after so the result is a number rather than an opinion.

  • Rotary brush and vacuum, not just a blower
  • Airflow reading before and after the work
  • Exterior termination cleared where access is safe
Learn About Dryer Vent Cleaning

Clogged Dryer Vent

Start here if clothes need two cycles, the laundry room runs hot, or there is no air at the outside hood. We trace the run and find where the air actually stops.

  • Duct run traced end to end
  • Restriction located before anything is cleaned
  • Escalated to blockage removal if the run is packed
Signs of a Clogged Vent

Dryer Vent Blockage Removal

For runs that are packed rather than dirty: compacted lint at an elbow, nesting material at a roof cap, or a collapsed section holding everything behind it.

  • Quoted after you have seen what is in there
  • Obstruction removed rather than pushed further in
  • Airflow verified once the run is open
About Blockage Removal

Roof Dryer Vent Cleaning

Interior and second-floor laundry rooms usually vent up through the attic to a roof cap. Longer runs, more turns, and the terminations nobody ever sees.

  • Cleaned from the laundry side with vacuum collection
  • Roof cap cleared where access is genuinely safe
  • What we could not reach is written down
About Roof Dryer Vents

Dryer Vent Repair

Crushed pipe, joints that pulled apart, leaking seams, a damaged hood, or a flap that will not swing. A clean duct with a crushed section still does not vent.

  • Fault shown to you in photos before it is quoted
  • Reachable sections repaired and resealed
  • Airflow re-measured to confirm the fix
About Dryer Vent Repair

Dryer Vent Replacement

When patching stops making sense. Foil or vinyl duct, a section crushed more than once, or a run with a leak at every joint is better replaced than repaired again.

  • Rigid or semi-rigid metal at the correct diameter
  • Joints fastened mechanically, run supported
  • Only the sections that need it, not the whole house
About Replacement

Dryer Vent Reconnection

Duct that has come apart inside an attic or wall is venting hot, damp air into the structure. Near-zero airflow at the hood with a dryer that seems fine is the signature.

  • Break located by tracing the run from both ends
  • Joint reseated, fastened and supported
  • Remaining reachable joints checked too
About Reconnection

Dryer Transition Hose Replacement

The short connector behind the machine is the cheapest part of the system and the most frequently crushed. Two feet of folded hose can undo thirty feet of clean duct.

  • Semi-rigid metal, cut to the shortest workable length
  • Clamped at both ends rather than taped
  • Dryer reseated so it is not crushed again
About Transition Hoses

Dryer Booster Fan Service

Long runs are sometimes built with an in-line booster fan. Sitting in the lint stream, it loads up and quietly stops helping, and nothing in the house tells you.

  • Located during the duct trace
  • Checked that it starts and stops with the dryer
  • Housing and impeller cleaned; no electrical work
About Booster Fans

Dryer Vent Airflow Testing

A measured reading and a visual check of the accessible components, with a written report. Useful when you want to know whether the duct is genuinely the problem.

  • Free and no obligation
  • A measured number, not a verbal impression
  • Not a government, code-compliance or certified fire inspection
About Airflow Testing
Which One Do I Need?

Pick by Symptom, Not by Service Name

Most people do not know which of the above they are looking at, and there is no reason they should. Match the symptom instead.

Clothes take two cycles, or the laundry room is hot +

That is the classic restriction pattern. Start with clogged dryer vent — we trace the run, find the restriction, and tell you whether a cleaning handles it or whether it has gone further than that.

There is no air at all coming out of the outside hood +

Not weak — nothing. That usually means the duct has come apart rather than clogged up, which is dryer vent reconnection. It is worth dealing with sooner than a clog, because the exhaust is going into your attic or wall instead.

The hose behind my dryer is ribbed silver or white plastic +

That is the connector, not the duct, and neither of those materials belongs there. Transition hose replacement is a small job that often fixes more airflow than people expect.

My laundry room is in the middle of the house or upstairs +

Then your dryer almost certainly vents through the attic to a cap on the roof. Roof dryer vent cleaning covers those runs, and if yours is long it may also have a booster fan you have never seen.

Someone pushed the dryer back and now it dries worse +

Almost always a crushed connector or a kinked section of duct. Dryer vent repair if the pipe is sound apart from the damage; replacement if it has been flattened more than once.

Nothing is obviously wrong, I just want to know where I stand +

Have the airflow tested. It is free, it takes under half an hour, and a measured number tells you whether anything needs doing at all.

Where We Stop

What We Do Not Do

We used to offer air duct cleaning, UV light systems and HVAC sanitization. We do not any more. Dryer vents are what we are good at, and a company that does one thing properly is more useful to you than one that does five things adequately.

  • No HVAC or air duct work. Supply and return ductwork, registers, coils and air handlers are outside what we offer.
  • No mould assessment or remediation. We will show you what we can see and leave the assessment to the licensed disciplines that do it.
  • No appliance repair. Heating elements, drums, belts, control boards and gas connections need an appliance technician.
  • No electrical or construction work. That includes wiring booster fans, cutting new exterior openings, and opening finished surfaces to reach buried duct.

If what you need is one of those, say so when you call and we will tell you plainly that it is not us — which is quicker for both of us than a visit that ends the same way.
