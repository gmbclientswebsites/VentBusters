Dryer Transition Hose Replacement

The short flexible hose between your dryer and the wall is the cheapest part of the system and the one most likely to be strangling it. It gets crushed every time the machine is pushed back, and on a lot of the homes we visit it is the whole problem.

The Part Everyone Forgets

Two Feet of Hose Can Undo Thirty Feet of Clean Duct

The transition hose exists because the dryer has to move. It connects the appliance outlet to the fixed duct in the wall, and it has to flex enough to let the machine be pulled out and pushed back. That flexibility is also its weakness.

Push a dryer back against the wall and the hose behind it folds. Fold it often enough and it stays folded. We routinely find an otherwise clean duct run choked at the very first eighteen inches, and the airflow reading cannot tell the difference between that and a plug of lint thirty feet away — which is exactly why we trace the run rather than guessing.

Length makes it worse. A hose left long because the installer had a long one in the van loops and sags behind the appliance, and every loop collects lint. Shorter is better, and the correct answer is usually the shortest length that still lets the machine be moved.

When It Needs Replacing

Six Reasons to Change the Connector

Most of these are visible in thirty seconds once the dryer is pulled forward.

💥

Crushed or folded flat

The commonest fault of all. A kink that has set will not open back up, and the restriction stays whether the duct is clean or not.

⚠️

Vinyl or plastic hose

Thin, often white or clear. It should not be carrying dryer exhaust and we will tell you so plainly.

🍵

Ribbed foil hose

Every ridge catches lint and it crushes under its own weight. Semi-rigid metal does the same job without either problem.

📏

Far too long

Excess length loops behind the machine, sags, and collects lint in the low points. Shortening it is often all that is needed.

💧

Torn or split

A tear means lint and moist air entering the laundry room instead of the duct, which you will notice as dust on nearby surfaces.

🔗

Loose at either end

A connection held by a single wrap of old tape. It will separate eventually, usually the next time the dryer is moved.

How We Do It

A Small Job, Done Properly

This is often bundled into a cleaning visit, and it takes very little time on its own.

1

Pull the machine forward safely

We check how the dryer is installed first — stacked units, strapped units and tight closets all change the answer — and we do not force anything that does not want to move.

2

Assess the existing connector

Material, length, condition, and how both ends are fastened. We show you what is back there, because most people have never seen it.

3

Fit semi-rigid metal, kept short

Cut to the shortest length that still lets the dryer be pulled out, clamped at both ends, and routed so it cannot fold when the machine goes back.

4

Reseat the dryer and verify

The machine goes back carefully so the new connector is not crushed on the way in, then we run the dryer and take an airflow reading.

Scope

What This Visit Covers

A small, well-defined job. The value is in doing the reseating properly, which is where the old one usually died.

  • Safely moving the dryer, including stacked and closet installations
  • Removing the existing transition hose and showing you its condition
  • New semi-rigid metal connector, cut to an appropriate length
  • Both ends clamped mechanically rather than taped
  • The dryer reseated so the connector is not crushed or kinked
  • Airflow reading after the work

What this visit does not cover

  • No gas line work. On a gas dryer we do not disconnect or reconnect the fuel supply. Where that is needed, it has to be done by someone licensed for it.
  • No appliance repair. We connect the dryer; we do not service what is inside it.
  • No forcing a fixed installation. If a stacked or built-in unit cannot be moved without risk, we stop and tell you.
  • No cabinetry modification. If the enclosure is too tight for a sound connection, that is a carpentry question.

What changes the price

Mostly access. A freestanding dryer in a garage is minutes. A stacked unit strapped into a closet with the connection behind it takes longer and more care, and we would rather take the time than break something.

It is frequently cheapest as part of a dryer vent cleaning visit, since the machine has to be pulled forward for that anyway. Ask when you book and we will tell you what the combined price looks like.

Questions

Transition Hose Replacement FAQ

Can I replace the transition hose myself? +

On a freestanding electric dryer with easy access, many people do. The parts are inexpensive and sold at any hardware store. The two things that go wrong are buying foil or vinyl because it is on the same shelf, and crushing the new one while pushing the machine back — which is exactly how the old one failed.

What is the difference between a transition hose and the duct? +

The transition hose is the short flexible connector between the appliance and the wall, and it is meant to be replaceable. The duct is the fixed run inside the building that carries the exhaust to the outdoors. Different parts, different lifespans, different jobs.

How short should it be? +

As short as it can be while still letting the dryer be pulled out for service. Excess length has no benefit and several costs: it sags, it loops, and the low points collect lint. We cut to fit rather than fitting whatever length comes in the box.

Is foil hose actually a problem if it is not crushed? +

It is not ideal even in good condition. The ribbed interior catches lint that a smooth wall would pass along, and it has no structural strength, so it is one careless push away from being crushed. Appliance instructions call for rigid or semi-rigid metal, and that is what we fit.

Will this on its own fix my drying times? +

Sometimes, completely — if a crushed connector was the only restriction, the change can be immediate and obvious. Often it is one of two or three things. The airflow reading before and after will tell you how much of the problem it was, which is better than either of us speculating.

Have You Looked Behind Your Dryer Lately?

If the hose back there is ribbed silver, white plastic, or folded over on itself, that is worth a conversation.
