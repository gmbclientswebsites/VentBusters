Is Duct Cleaning a Scam? What to Actually Look For

Honest answer: the skepticism is earned. Duct cleaning has one of the worst reputations in home services, and it's not undeserved — the industry has a long history of lowball ads, high-pressure upsells, and companies that do more harm than good. Here's how to tell the difference.

Why the Industry Has a Bad Name

You've probably seen the ads: "Whole House Duct Cleaning — $99!" That price almost never survives contact with your actual home. The technician shows up, "discovers" your system needs far more work, and pressures you into paying several times the advertised rate on the spot — or does a rushed, surface-level job that doesn't actually clean anything.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • An exact price quoted sight-unseen. No legitimate technician can accurately price a job without seeing your system first.
  • High-pressure, same-visit upsells. "Your system is dangerously moldy, we need to start right now" with no time to think it over is a classic script.
  • No license or insurance information offered. A legitimate company will hand this over without hesitation.
  • Refuses to show you the problem. If a technician claims your ducts are filthy but won't show you photos or let you look, that's worth questioning.
  • No real online presence. No reviews, no verifiable address, no way to check who you're actually letting into your house.

What Legitimate Service Actually Looks Like

A trustworthy technician inspects first and explains what they find in plain language — including telling you when nothing needs to be done. You get to see the evidence, not just take their word for it. Pricing is based on your actual system, not a bait-and-switch number from a flyer. And there's zero pressure to decide on the spot.

If a company won't show you the problem, won't give you time to think, or already knows the price before walking in your door — that's not an inspection, it's a script.
