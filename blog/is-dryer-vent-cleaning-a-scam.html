Is Dryer Vent Cleaning a Scam?

It is a fair question, and we would rather answer it honestly than be offended by it. Dryer vent cleaning is real work on a real problem with a result you can measure. The reason people ask is that the trade has a low barrier to entry, no licensing requirement in Florida, and a well-established set of tactics for turning a cheap advertised price into an expensive invoice.

The Short Answer

No — the service is legitimate. A dryer vent genuinely fills with lint, a restricted vent genuinely makes the appliance run longer and hotter, and failure to clean was the leading factor in home clothes-dryer fires analysed by the U.S. Fire Administration. Removing the lint and restoring the airflow is useful work, and the before-and-after airflow reading proves whether it happened.

What is sometimes a scam is the way it gets sold. Those are different problems with different answers.

Why the Question Exists

Bait pricing

A headline price low enough that nobody could do the work for it, designed to get a technician into your laundry room. Once the dryer is pulled out and you are committed, the price is revised — often with a description of your duct alarming enough to justify it. The tell is a firm quote given with no questions asked about your run, because a real quote depends on length, turns and access.

Blower-only "cleaning"

A leaf blower held at one end of the duct for ninety seconds. It moves some lint and makes a mess, and it leaves the compacted material at the bends exactly where it was. Proper cleaning drives a rotary brush through the run while a vacuum captures what it frees, so the lint leaves the system rather than relocating inside it.

Scare tactics

Fire statistics are real and worth knowing. Using them as a pressure device is not. If somebody is in your house telling you the dryer is a fire waiting to happen and the price for averting it goes up as the story gets worse, that is sales, not safety. Nobody can honestly tell you a cleaning prevents fires, only that it removes fuel and restores airflow.

Add-ons for problems you do not have

Sanitising, deodorising, or treating a dryer exhaust for mould. A dryer duct moves hot air out of the house; it is not the part of your home where these services make sense. We do not offer them, and we would be sceptical of anyone selling them on a laundry vent.

Counting vents that are not dryer vents

You have one dryer vent. A per-vent price multiplied by every register in the house is quoting a different job from the one you asked about.

What a Legitimate Job Looks Like

  • They ask questions first. Where the laundry room is, which floor, where it vents out. That is how a quote gets anchored to your actual run.
  • They trace the run before cleaning it. Clearing a vent without knowing where the restriction is, is guesswork.
  • They measure airflow before and after. Two numbers, taken the same way, is the only honest proof the work changed anything.
  • Brush and vacuum, not just air. The lint should leave the duct, not move further along it.
  • They tell you what they could not reach. Duct behind drywall, a roof cap that was not safe to work, a stacked unit that would not move. Scope limits belong in the summary.
  • Repairs are quoted separately, with photographs. If the duct is crushed or separated, cleaning will not fix the airflow, and a company that cleans it anyway and hands you a bill has not solved your problem.

Six Questions to Ask Before Booking

  • Do you measure airflow before and after? If not, ask how you will know it worked.
  • Do you use a rotary brush and a vacuum? Listen for both, not just one.
  • What would make the price change once you are here? A straight answer is a good sign; vagueness is not.
  • What do you do if the duct turns out to be damaged? The right answer involves showing you and quoting it separately.
  • Will you get to the roof cap, and what if you cannot? Honest companies have a policy about unsafe access.
  • Are you insured? Worth asking. Note that dryer vent cleaning is not a licensed trade in Florida, so anyone claiming to be a licensed or certified dryer vent cleaner is describing something that does not exist.

What to Ask For Afterwards

Two things: the airflow readings, and the photographs. The readings tell you whether the restriction was removed. The photographs tell you what was in there and what it looks like now. Any company doing the work properly will have both, because taking them is part of the job rather than an extra.

The service is not the scam. The absence of measurement is what makes a scam possible, because without a number before and after, there is nothing to check.

Where We Stand

We clean dryer vents and we repair them, and that is the entire list. We do not do air duct work, UV lights or sanitising, we do not describe ourselves as licensed or certified, and we do not claim any service prevents fires. What we do is take an airflow reading before we start, clear the accessible run with brush and vacuum, measure again, and show you both numbers and the photographs. If that is not what you are being offered elsewhere, that is the thing worth asking about.

If you would rather find out where your run stands before committing to anything, the airflow test is free and carries no obligation.
